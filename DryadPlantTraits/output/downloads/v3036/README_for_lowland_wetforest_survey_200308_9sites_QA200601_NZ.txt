Institute of Pacific Islands ForestryUS Forest ServiceInvasive Species TeamDraft: January 15, 2006Data Documentation Protocol:Data Set name: lowland_wetforest_survey_200308_9sites_QA200601_NZ.xlsWhy the data were collected: To assess the succession and invasive species trends across a chronosequence of largely intact primary succession lowland wet forests in Hawaii ranging from ca. 50 to ca. 2500 years old.  This will then be used to better understand how these interactions vary from site to site and over time.  Such information will be useful in further understanding the impact of invasive species in Hawaii's rare native lowland wet forests.  Data was collected at nine study sites, differentiated by substrate type and flow age: 2 sites in the Malama Ki Forest Reserve (MKO and MKY), 3 sites in the Nanawale Forest Reserve (NAN, NAN47, NANO), 2 sites in the Keauohana Forest Reserve (KOY and KOM), one site in the Keaukaha Military Reserve (KMR), and one on Kamehameha Schools Bishop Estate land at Pu'u Kaliu (BRY).  The woody Keauohana data was contributed from a paper by Flint Hughes et al.What data were collected: Transects were established at the site if not already present, and a concentric nested plot design was used to gather data.  Diameter at Breast Height (DBH, ca. ~1.3 m) data was collected at seven study sites (all sites excluding the two at Keauohana) for woody stems of diameter greater than 2 cm and less than 30 cm at breast height within a circular plot with 9 m radius, and each stem was identified to species.  Within a concentric plot of radius 18 m, all stems with diameter at breast height greater than 30 cm were also measured and identified to species. Multiple stems of the same individual were discerned whenever possible and recorded in the data.  This was done by grouping (drawing a circle around) multiple stems of the same individual on the data sheet.  Saplings (considered those woody stems less than 2 cm DBH but total height greater than breast height) were measured within yet another concentric plot of radius 6m.  Saplings were tallied and identified to species, but DBH data was not collected, and incidences of multiple stems were not recorded.  At the two Keauohana sites, a similar but not identical methodology was used.  At the Keauohana sites, woody species data (no size class constraints other than that stems had to be greater than 2 cm DBH) was measured within a radius of 5.642 m, and sapling data within a radius of 2.821 m. Herbaceous data was also collected at each of the sites (all sites had identical herbaceous methodology).  The top-most layer lower than breast height was tallied using the point-quadrat method (recorded the type of cover directly beneath each of 81 equally spaces points within a 1 m2 quadrat).  This was repeated 4 times at each plot: 3 m South, 6 m East, 9 m North, and 12 m West from the center point of the plot.  The average of these four quadrats is what appears in the data set.What instrument and source were used: Standard DBH tapes were used to measure DBH data.  30 m self-retracting foresters tapes were used to measure the concentric plots.  The tape was attached to the belt of the person measuring and the end of it to the center point of each plot.  This made it possible to determine if species were within each of the respective plots, even in dense forest.  After woody species and saplings were measured, they were marked with forestry crayon to prevent double-countingWho collected the data and who should be contacted with questions: Naupaka Zimmerman, David Perez, Kalei Chang, Ryan Like, Flint Hughes, Kaiena David Bishaw, Rebecca Ostertag, Susan Cordell, Patrick Hart, Amanda Uowolo, and Cheyenne Perry all contributed to data collection.  Naupaka Zimmerman should be contacted with any questions regarding methodology used or the resultant data at (808) 989-4009 or at naupaka@post.harvard.edu.Who funded the investigation: USDA Forest Service and the Research Corporation of the University of Hawaii.	The name(s) of the data file(s) in the data set: lowland_wetforest_survey_200308_9sites_QA200601_NZ.xlsHow should the data be cited: The data should be cited from any publication containing the data.Where and with what spatial resolution were the data collected: All woody or liana stems that were > 2 cm and < 30 cm DBH (ca. 1.37m) and were > 45 degrees of vertical were identified and measured within a 9 m radius, stems > 30 cm DBH within a 18 m radius, and saplings were tallied within a 6 m radius.  Data was collected at nine study sites, differentiated by substrate type and flow age: 2 sites in the Malama Ki Forest Reserve (MKO, 10 plots sampled and MKY, 10 plots sampled), 3 sites in the Nanawale Forest Reserve (NAN, 11 plots sampled, NAN47, 10 plots sampled, NANO, 10 plots sampled), 2 sites in the Keauohana Forest Reserve (KOY, 10 plots sampled, and KOM, 10 plots sampled), one site in the Keaukaha Military Reserve (KMR, 10 plots sampled), and one on Kamehameha Schools Bishop Estate land at Pu'u Kaliu (BRY, 13 plots sampled).  Description of study area: The study sites for the Lowland Wet Forest Project are all below 2500 ft (the cutoff for lowland wet forests, as recently defined by the Lowland Wet Forest Working Group), although the majority of them are at or below 100 ft elevation.  They are all located in the Puna District on the Big Island of Hawaii.  They all have a wet moisture regime and have a largely intact native overstory.When and how frequently were the data collected: The data was collected during Summer 2003 and Summer 2004. Field protocol for data collection: Standard DBH tapes were used to measure stems that had a diameter greater than 2 cm.  Stems had to be at a vertical angle > 45 degrees, stem had to originate below DBH, be rooted within the plot, and fit the size criteria of 2.0 cm or greater DBH in order to be counted.  For sapling-type data, if any extent of the plant reached above breast height (~1.37 m), then it was tallied.  These stems also had to have a main stem at >45 degrees.  Herbaceous data was tallied using the point-quadrat method for ground cover estimation.  Only the topmost living herbaceous layer was counted below each point, up to a maximum height of breast height (ca. 1.3 m).What was each parameter measured or produced; what are its units of measure; what format was used for the parameters in the data set; what is the precision and accuracy of each parameter; and what is the relationship to other data in the data set: DBH data was collected for stems that met the proper criteria.  The unit of measure for the DBH data is centimeters. The precision of the DBH data is to the nearest 0.1 cm.  The sapling tallies were stem counts of each species that met the specified criteria.  The herbaceous tallies were recorded as tallies out of 81 points, and then converted to an average percent cover. What data processing procedures were used including screening for errors: Data was recoded on write-in-the-rain paper in the field and then transferred into Microsoft Excel for data analysis.  This data entry was then blind checked twice to catch any input errors.What software was used to prepare the data set: Microsoft Excel 2004 What special codes, including those for missing values or for stations used: Sapling data do not have DBH measurements; for the sapling entries, those cells are empty.  Herbaceous data also lacks these measurements, those cell are empty as well.  When were the data last modified: January 15, 2006Summary statistics generated from the final file: An example file record: PLOT_TYPE,NAME,COMMON_NAME,SITE,PLOT,X__INDIV,DBH__CM_,BASAL_AREA_M2,INDIV_TREE_BA_M2,BA_PER_HA_M2,NUM_INDIV_PER_HA,TALLY_OUT_AVG,PERCENT_COVERherb,Cyrtandra sp.,,BRY,1,,,,,,,7.25,8.9519,Metrosideros polymorpha ,('ohi'a),NAN,3,1,8.4,0.00554,0.00554,0.21778,39.29752Names or locations of pertinent field notes or companion files: Hardcopies of the data as well as summary statistics and preliminary analysis are kept by Flint Hughes.Names of related or ancillary data sets: Known problems that limit data use: Several species could not be precisely identified to the species level, these were left identified to genus and grouped together to prevent erroneous labeling.
